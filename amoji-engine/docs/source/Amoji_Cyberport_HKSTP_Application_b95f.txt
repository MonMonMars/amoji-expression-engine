AMOJI
A Bridge Between AI and Human — Technology Incubation Application
1. Executive Summary
Amoji is a rule-based expression and behavior engine that converts AI-generated emotional intent into full facial, body, and gesture performance data for digital characters and robots. Built on 20 years of professional character animation expertise combined with published psychology, performance-capture, and human-computer-interaction research, Amoji addresses a gap in the current AI-character market: existing systems either detect human emotion or apply generic, static expression templates — very few generate psychologically grounded, personality-consistent performance.
The engine is architecturally separated from any biometric user-detection function, positioning it as a pure generation layer that is licensable to LLM platforms, robotics manufacturers, and consumer application developers without inheriting the regulatory burden associated with emotion-detection systems.
2. Problem & Solution
The Problem
AI characters, digital humans, and social robots increasingly need to express emotion, but most current systems produce flat, robotic, or inconsistent performance — particularly during conversational pauses, emotional transitions, and AI processing delays. Recent high-profile robotics launches (2025-2026) have received mixed critical reception specifically on this point, with some described as "climbing out of" the uncanny valley and others as "unsettling" despite similar underlying hardware specifications.
The Solution
Amoji provides a layered, deterministic engine spanning: compound emotion generation (region-specific blending rather than naive averaging), a background "mood" system that subtly biases displayed emotion over time, natural micro-expression timing, eye-gaze and micro-saccade behavior, breathing and postural systems, gesture layering, gait, and a persona parameter system that mathematically derives behavioral limits for any character archetype — human, animal, anthropomorphized object, or mechanical robot — without per-character redesign.
3. Technology & Innovation
The engine's architecture separates an abstract "emotion intensity" layer from a concrete "rendering" layer, allowing the identical decision logic to drive outputs ranging from a minimal glowing-orb interface (comparable to existing voice-assistant UI) through stylized 2D/3D characters to photorealistic digital humans and physical robot actuators — a single backend, fully pluggable presentation layer.
Core technical assets (summarized; full specification retained as trade secret):
A 77-point facial expression system and 117-point full-body marker system, each cross-referenced to specific anatomical muscle groups and nerve pathways
A compound-emotion engine grounded in published affective-science research (Du & Martinez, 2014) rather than linear parameter averaging
A viseme (lip-sync) override architecture resolving conflicts between speech articulation and emotional expression using a locked/clamped/open state model, addressing a documented weakness in prior industry dominance models
A "character archetype" adapter layer generalizing the engine beyond human faces to animals, anthropomorphized objects, and mechanical systems
Built-in compliance modules for AI-disclosure, anti-addiction, and crisis-intervention behavior, designed against current EU, US, and Mainland China regulatory requirements for anthropomorphic AI interaction services
The engine deliberately uses deterministic, auditable rule logic rather than a black-box machine-learning model for the core expression-generation step, prioritizing reproducibility, regulatory auditability, and defensible intellectual property over a purely data-driven approach.
4. Market Opportunity
The market for expressive AI characters and social robots has accelerated sharply through 2025-2026, with multiple manufacturers — spanning Mainland China, the UK, and North America — shipping or piloting hyper-realistic humanoid and desktop robot products aimed at elder-care companionship, hospitality, education, and customer service. Several of these products are hardware- and base-AI-strong but lack a differentiated, research-grounded expression layer, representing a direct B2B licensing opportunity.
Separately, major LLM platforms have begun shipping animated 3D companion features directly to consumers, with public reception noting a lack of character customization depth and emotional consistency — a gap Amoji's architecture is designed to address.

7. Development Status
The engine's full architecture specification is complete, spanning facial and body anatomy, compound emotion logic, mood modeling, eye/gaze systems, gait, gesture, viseme coarticulation, multi-species/object adaptation, and uncanny-valley risk management, informed by both academic literature and current industry case studies.
